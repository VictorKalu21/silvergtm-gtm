Append-only. **What works, by campaign motion × vertical.** Read BEFORE a review to set priors; append AFTER (see SKILL.md → Self-improvement protocol). When priors disagree with an account's own data, the account's data wins — then update the row.

Two motions, classify every campaign first:
- **Buyer-acquisition** — prospect is sold something to *buy* (default: SaaS, services, sponsorship, ads).
- **Seller-sourcing** — prospect is asked to *sell/exit/list* (M&A, "we buy X", supply-side). Timing-gated; only active sellers bite.

## META-PATTERNS (hold across verticals — the highest-value lessons)

1. **Reply-type signature reveals buyer sophistication — and it dictates the CTA.**
   - **Owner-operator verticals** (healthcare practices, home services, local trades, solo shops): the owner reads email personally → replies are **"what's the cost?" / "call me, here's my cell" / "chat next week."** These are **call-ready and HOT** — one step from booked. → **Phone/call CTA wins.** Owner answers the phone.
   - **Marketing-function verticals** (high-ticket retail, agencies, mid-market): a marketing person / ad budget exists → replies are **"send me the media package" / "questions about brand alignment" / routed to "our CMO."** These evaluate the offer as a *media buy* — **cooler and considered**, and a "send it" soft-yes **stalls at PDF-dispensing** unless the follow-up explicitly bridges to a call. → **Media-kit-then-call-bridge**, never treat "send it" as the win.
   - *Corollary:* "call me" (owner-operator) is a **better** reply than "send me the deck" (marketing-function), even though the latter looks more engaged. Don't mistake reply *politeness/length* for reply *heat*.

1b. **CTA hierarchy — CONFIRMED across both LNP verticals (the direct time-bound ask wins).** Ranked by reply rate: **"When's the best time for a chat this week?" (direct, time-bound meeting ask) = clear OUTPERFORMER** — top variant in both campaigns (RT 7.5% / HC 4.4%) + both winning E3s (6.3-7.2%). **Failures:** "Are you the right person/contact?" as the CLOSING line (0-3.9% — fine mid-body, tanks as the closer); "Open to grabbing a slot?" (vague, 0-2%); statement with no question (0%). **Mid:** "Worth a look?" (best for the E2 follow-up slot specifically, ~3%), "Got 10 min to talk?" (~3%). **Controlled proof it's the CTA not the vertical:** retail E3-C ends on "right person?" (3.9%) while same-step E3-A/B append "…chat this week?" → jumps to 6.3-7.2%. Note: the direct-chat CTA is caller-INDEPENDENT (prospect proposes the time, you take a scheduled call) — right even when the client has no dialer team; a literal phone CTA ("can I call you?") is the wrong move with no callers.

2. **"Send me info / the one-pager / the deck" is a soft-yes that stalls at PDF.** Always bridge: sent-the-thing → "15-min walk-through." (Bluesoft killed its own bookings by making the CTA *"can I send the one pager?"* — a yes became a PDF request, conversation over.)

3. **Follow-ups earn ~half the replies; the breakup/final email is often a TOP producer.** Don't shorten sequences. (LNP healthcare steps 2-3 = 48% of replies; LNP retail step-3 *out-pulled* the opener.)

4. **Short curiosity subjects beat clever/mechanism subjects — CONFIRMED across 2 verticals (identical hierarchy).** Same rank order in LNP healthcare AND retail: **Email-1 "Quick question" wins** (health 4.4% / retail 7.5%); **Email-3 "about {{company_name}}" wins** (2.9% / 7.2%, also honest as a cold open); **"{{first_name}}?" is dead** (0% / 3.9% — 0 replies in 107 healthcare sends); **"beyond Google?" is the worst Email-1** (1.9% / 0%). Cross-vertical consistency = trust this over any single-campaign number. Also note: blank-subject threaded follow-ups (the classic "RE:… looks-like-a-convo" trick) did NOT beat the personalized actual-subject variant here — "about {{company_name}}" edged the threaded one at Email 3 in both (n small, treat ~tied).

5. **Speed-to-lead is the game for local/owner-operator.** Replies land <2h (median 1.5h) and 90%+ within 48h; hot ones want to book *today*. Pace small daily morning batches; work replies same-morning. Sending after ~1pm local is mostly wasted.

6. **Deliverability verdict = real signals only, never the domain TLD.** Judge by bounce %, auto-reply share, positive-rate collapse across varied copy, or literal "went to spam" replies. `.info`/`.co` lookalike domains land fine (LNP's whole fleet is `.info`/`.co`, mail lands, brand-recognized replies). [user directive 2026-07-02]

7. **Seller-sourcing needs a value-asset wedge, not a "let's talk deal" CTA.** (Minerva.)

8. **ROSTER SUCCESS PATTERN (2026-07-12, first cross-client success review, n=10 clients — pattern-reading, not statistics).** Booked-meetings-per-1,000-contacted spread ~20-80x across the book, and the winners share three traits while deliverability was FINE on the losers (bounce ~1-2%, mail lands — so the gap is client-fit, not ops):
   - **Winners (2-6 booked/1k):** LNP 5.6 (local owner-operator, Maps-verified lists, accelerating 3→11 booked/mo), Campus51 ~3-4 (founder-led private schools, offer converts, list hygiene was the only drag), Untitled email-era-2.4-blended (but its June-July booking stream = ZERO; early bookings predate current workspace), LinkHelpers 1.7 (local biz, first month).
   - **Losers (0.07-0.32 booked/1k):** SolveX 0.07, Bluesoft 0.11, Routify 0.27, Minerva 0.32 — all selling to non-local/committee/considered buyers off generic Apollo pulls.
   - The three shared winner traits: (a) **owner-operator/founder buyer** who reads own inbox; (b) **source-native list** (Maps-verified / directory / store-platform) — visible WITHIN accounts too: Bluesoft SI-store lists 1.6-3.1% reply vs Apollo 0.4-0.8%; Routify client-list/2-10-size campaigns 1.2-2% + 0.5% opp vs 0.1-0.2% Apollo blasts; Untitled Shopify-directory breakthrough; (c) **concrete easy-yes offer** (sponsorship slot, free workshop/needs-analysis, free demand map) vs abstract SaaS demo.
   - **Deal-model exposure:** the pay-per-appointment book (SolveX, Bluesoft, Minerva, Elite) produced 1-6 appointments each = barely-paid engagements; per-appointment pricing only pays where the profile scores high (LNP). KPI calibration was off on the SaaS book (Untitled 8/mo never hit, best=7; Visus 3-5/WEEK with zero logged).

### Outsourced finance / fractional-CFO → Professional-services firms (buyer-acquisition) — UPDATES the Minerva seller-sourcing row
